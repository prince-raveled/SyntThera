Connecting molecular signatures to drug discovery

Mimicking exercise through molecular discovery.

An AI search for medicines that do what a workout does.

Exercise protects against almost every long-term disease, yet many people cannot do it: after a spinal cord injury, with heart failure, in intensive care or with severe frailty. SynThera reads the molecular fingerprint of exercise in real people and searches 29,680 compounds for drugs that reproduce it.

    A map of 29,680 compounds.

    Each dot is a compound, placed by what its genes do according to the GSFM AI model, so drugs that act alike sit together. Colour shows how well each one matches the fingerprint picked in the explorer; diamonds are the exercise fingerprints themselves. Hover for names, click to look one up.

    Why human data

    Rats and people point to different drugs.

    Earlier searches, including a 2026 Bio-IT World hackathon project built on the same AI model, started from rat exercise data translated into human genes. We ran SynThera's identical pipeline on the MoTrPAC rat data to see whether the starting species matters.
